Cross-Country Car Shipping: What to Expect on a Long-Haul Move

Cost, timing, open vs. enclosed, and how to book without overpaying.

By Dana Mercer · August 2026

Shipping a car across the country is more straightforward than most people expect — but there are enough variables on a long-haul route that going in without a plan costs time and money. Here is what to know before you book.

What cross-country shipping actually costs

For most standard sedans and SUVs on a cross-country route (roughly 2,000 to 3,000 miles), open carrier transport runs between $900 and $1,400. Enclosed transport on the same route runs $1,200 to $2,000 or more, depending on the carrier and timing.

Cost per mile drops on longer routes. You will often pay more per mile for a 500-mile haul than a 2,500-mile one, because carriers can fill a full trailer on high-volume long-haul lanes and spread the cost across more vehicles.

The biggest swing factor after distance is seasonality. Shipping in January or February, when demand is lowest, can save $150 to $300 compared to peak summer rates. Snowbird migration (October through November southbound, March through April northbound) creates temporary price spikes on Florida and Southwest corridors.

High-volume corridors vs. remote routes

The most-traveled corridors — New York to Los Angeles, Chicago to Miami, Texas to California — have dense carrier availability and competitive pricing. Pickups on these routes happen relatively fast: 1 to 5 business days after booking for most companies.

Less-traveled routes (rural Midwest to Pacific Northwest, for example) can take longer to dispatch because fewer carriers run those lanes. If your origin or destination is a smaller city or rural area, factor in an extra 2 to 5 days for carrier assignment and be flexible on exact pickup dates.

How long a cross-country shipment takes

Typical coast-to-coast transit time is 7 to 10 days from pickup to delivery. Midwest to either coast usually runs 5 to 8 days. These are transit windows, not guarantees — weather, traffic, and multi-stop carrier routes affect timing.

If you need a car at a specific time, expedited shipping is available from most carriers for an additional $200 to $400. For most people, building a few extra days of buffer into the timeline is cheaper than paying for expedited service.

Open carrier vs. enclosed for a long haul

Open carrier is the standard choice for cross-country moves. The cars you see on highways strapped to multi-level open trailers are almost all on long-haul routes. For a standard daily driver, open carrier is sufficient.

Enclosed makes sense for long-haul moves when the vehicle has high value, low production numbers, or cosmetic condition that matters on arrival. The extra cost on a cross-country route — roughly $300 to $600 more than open carrier — buys protection from weather, road debris, and visibility during transit. Door-to-door vs. terminal shipping

Most reputable companies offer door-to-door service: the carrier picks up at your address and delivers to the destination address. In dense urban areas (New York, Chicago, Los Angeles), door-to-door is standard but can sometimes take an extra day because large car haulers have limited access on narrow city streets.

Terminal shipping — where you drop off and pick up at a carrier depot — is less common and usually not cheaper. Stick with door-to-door unless a company has a specific reason to recommend otherwise.

What to book and when

Book at least 2 weeks before your target pickup date for a standard move. 3 to 4 weeks out in peak season (May through September) gives you more flexibility and often better pricing. Booking too close to your date forces you to take whatever carrier is available at whatever price.

If you have a hard deadline — closing on a house, starting a job — tell your carrier and consider booking further in advance or paying for expedited priority.

The one thing that surprises most first-time shippers

Quotes vary significantly for the same route. A $200 gap between two companies for a cross-country move is common; sometimes the gap is larger. The company with the lowest quote is not always the one that shows up on the day you agreed on with a well-reviewed carrier.

Compare at least two or three quotes before booking, and look beyond price. Customer reviews, deposit policy, and whether the company has an in-house dispatch team or simply posts your vehicle on a shared marketplace and waits all affect how your shipment actually goes.
